Small build, one fix, and it is for Spanish.

There is a technique for spelling out loud that beats every other trick: naming the letter with an example word. In English, "B as in boy". It works because the phone cannot confuse "boy" with anything, even when it cannot tell B from P.

In Spanish the equivalent is "be de burro" — and it has never worked in this app. The connecting word, "de", is also the name of the letter D, so the app heard the letter D in the middle of your clarification and handed back "bd". The technique for making yourself understood made you less understood.

It matters more in Spanish than anywhere else, because b and v are the same sound in spoken Spanish. "Be de burro" and "uve de vaca" is how Spanish speakers have always told them apart out loud. That now works, and the hint telling you about it now appears in Spanish.

The part that took the longest was not breaking ordinary spelling while fixing it. Spelling "verde" out loud is "uve e ere de e" — there is "de" again, in the middle of a perfectly normal word, and an app that treats every "de" as a clarifier turns verde into ver. So the rule is narrow: a connecting word that is also a letter name only counts as one when what follows it is a real word rather than another letter.

What is most useful now:

• Spanish: try "be de burro" and "uve de vaca" on a word with b or v in it. Does the right letter land every time?
• Spanish: spell a word containing D normally — verde, dedo, donde — and check nothing goes missing.
• English: "B as in boy" is unchanged, and worth a try if a letter keeps coming out wrong.
• Everything from 271 and 272 still stands, especially spelling at your natural speed rather than slowing down.

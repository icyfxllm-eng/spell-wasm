Two ways to make spelling out loud actually work, one of which has been hiding in the app the whole time.

Some letters sound almost identical: B, C, D, E, G, P, T, V and Z are nearly the same sound with a different consonant stuck on the front. Phones are poor at telling them apart, and that is most of what goes wrong when you spell a word out loud.

There is an old fix for this, and the app has quietly supported it since the beginning: say "B as in boy". The letter is taken and the example word is thrown away. It has always worked and nothing ever told you about it, so nobody used the one thing that solves the hardest part. It is in the how-to now.

The second is the alphabet pilots and radio operators use: Alpha, Bravo, Charlie, Delta. Those words were chosen precisely because they survive a bad connection, and they work far better than single letters. You can now spell with them — "Charlie, Alfa, Tango" gives you cat — and you can mix them freely with ordinary letters in the same breath. Use them for the letters that give you trouble and plain letters for the rest.

Both of these are English for now. The equivalent phrasing in Spanish turned out not to work — in Spanish the connecting word is itself the name of a letter, so the app hears the connector and loses the letter you wanted. Rather than teach you a phrase that fails, the tip stays hidden in Spanish until that is fixed properly.

What is most useful now:

• Try "B as in boy" on a letter that keeps coming out wrong. Does it land every time?
• Try "Charlie Alfa Tango" and mixing styles mid-word.
• Tell me which letters still fail most often. That list is what the next improvement gets aimed at.
• If you spell out loud in a language other than English or Spanish, errors are still expected — those letter names were drafted by machine and have not been checked by a speaker.

Two real changes this time, and both are about being marked wrong when you were right.

If you spell in British, Australian, Indian or Canadian English, the game used to mark you wrong for spelling normally. Type favourite, colour, neighbour, licence, recognise or travelling and it counted as a miss, because only the American spelling was in the word list. Those spellings are now accepted either way — twenty-odd pairs, wherever the two spellings sound identical. The word you hear is the same word; there was never a way to tell from the audio which spelling was wanted, so marking one of them wrong was our bug, not your mistake.

The English voice has also changed. We put nine candidate voices through both speech recognizers on 68 words, and the one we were using came sixth. It was the reason half sounded like "have", leaf like "leave" and safe like "save" — a word-final "f" being read as a "v". The new voice gets all three right. Every English word is being re-recorded with it, so if a word sounds slightly different from yesterday, that is why.

This is the fix for the problem two of you reported. Thank you — it took a tester noticing to find it, and then a machine test to prove it was the voice rather than the recording.

What is most useful now:

• Does the new English voice sound clearer to you, or just different? Either answer is useful.
• If you spell in a non-American English, does anything still get marked wrong that should not?
• "Can't hear it" is in SpellDoku, Spell Search, Spell Cross and Spell Picture.

If a word is still hard to make out after hearing it slowly, tell us the word and the language. That is how this one was found.

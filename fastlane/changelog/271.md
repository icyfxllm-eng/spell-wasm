Spelling out loud, part two. 270 got the letters into the box; this one is about the words that still came out wrong.

Three things were losing letters, and all three are fixed.

Doubled letters. Spell "little" and the phone would give you one T, or three. It was guessing where each letter ended using a stopwatch, which cannot tell a deliberate second T from the phone still making its mind up about the first. It now uses the pause it actually measured. Both T's and both L's land.

Noisy rooms. The microphone works out how loud "speech" is by listening to the room, and after the last build it was not allowed to set that bar high enough — so in a room with any background noise, your voice and the room looked the same to it, letters ran together and some vanished. The bar is now set relative to how loud you actually are, rather than to a number chosen in a quiet room.

Letters run together. Spell "bumped" quickly and the phone would write your U and M as the word "UM", which is not a letter, so it threw the whole thing away and you lost the M. Same for the K of "book", which it kept writing as "OK". Those are now read as the letters they are.

And something new: when the phone genuinely cannot tell two letters apart, it asks instead of guessing. B, C, D, E, G, P, T, V and Z are the same sound with a different consonant on the front, and M and N are closer still. If it is unsure, two letters appear under your spelling and you tap the one you said. If you ignore it and carry on, it takes its best guess — exactly what it would have done before — so it can never slow you down or cost you a letter.

What is most useful now:

• Spell at the speed that feels natural, especially if that is fast. Deliberate spelling is well tested; quick spelling is not, and it is the thing I most need to hear about. If letters merge or go missing when you speed up, tell me the word.
• Double letters: little, book, better, running. Do both land?
• If the two-letter chooser appears, was it right to ask? Appearing when you spoke clearly is just as much a problem as not appearing when you did not.
• Noisy room, quiet room, headphones, speakerphone. Any difference is worth knowing.

Still wrong, and known: if the phone is confidently wrong — it decides you said P when you said B and is sure about it — nothing here catches that, because the chooser only appears when it is unsure. "B as in boy" and the Bravo/Charlie/Delta alphabet both still work and both sidestep it entirely.

The word lists got a hard clean, German nouns got their capital letters back, and about 3,600 more words have a definition.

This is the biggest change to the word lists the app has had. 9,340 entries came out across thirteen languages — words that were not really words of that language, misspellings, fragments, and names that had drifted in from the text the lists were built from. Korean lost more than half of its list. Japanese, Filipino and Arabic lost a large share too.

  ko 6,233 to 2,896      ja 6,160 to 4,979      fil 4,083 to 3,225
  ar 6,238 to 5,526      hi 2,674 to 2,095      pt 6,110 to 5,525
  fr 6,109 to 5,543      es 6,097 to 5,632      de 6,144 to 5,734

A shorter list of real words is better than a long list with rubbish in it, but a cut this size is exactly the kind of thing that looks fine in a spreadsheet and feels wrong in the hand. That is the main thing to test in this build.

German nouns are now capitalised — 1,710 of them. German capitalises every noun, and the list had them in lower case, which simply spells them wrong. Haus, not haus.

That fix had a payoff nobody planned. A dictionary indexes German nouns under their capital, so correcting the spelling made 1,453 German words findable that had never had a definition.

Separately, about 1,900 words across Spanish, French, Portuguese, Swahili, English and German gained a definition through a different route. When a dictionary is asked about "textes" it answers "plural of texte" — true, and useless as a hint, so the app used to throw it away. It now follows the pointer and shows "plural: text" instead. Definitions altogether went from 44,729 to 48,358. These arrive from the server rather than this build, so they are already there.

Vietnamese deliberately gained nothing from that second route. Its dictionary pointers are nearly all "alternative spelling of", and in a spelling game that means two correct spellings behind one meaning, which is the last thing this app should teach.

Finally, Settings has a Notices row. It lists the open datasets and typefaces the app is built on — the word lists come from Wikipedia text via a public corpus, the definitions from Wiktionary — and several of those ask to be credited. The page existed in the project but had never shipped, so nobody could read it. Now it opens.


What is most useful now:

• Korean, above everything else. The list is less than half what it was. Play all four levels and tell me whether it still feels like a full game or like it is running out of words.
• Japanese, Filipino, Arabic, Hindi: same question, smaller cut.
• German: a noun should now come up as Haus rather than haus. Check that answering works the way you expect, and tell me if capitalisation makes a word feel wrong to type.
• Any language: if a word you know is REAL has gone missing, that is the most valuable thing you can report. The sweep was deliberately aggressive and will have taken some good words with the bad.
• Spanish, French, Portuguese, German: tap Definition on a plural or past-tense word. It should answer now, and say which form it is.
• Settings → Notices: just confirm it opens and reads properly.

New here? Tap the glowing orb to hear a word, then spell it on the keyboard and press Check. Everything works offline after the first load.

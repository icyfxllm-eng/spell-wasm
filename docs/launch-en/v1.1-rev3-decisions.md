## Near-sound gate (F14)

- **D-NS0.** A near-sound answer is never marked correct. True homophones stay with SENSE-CUE. This replaces rev 2's pointer to "VOICE-CLARITY D1", which is now in the repo at `docs/cc/CC-VOICE-CLARITY-v1.md` and says the same thing.
- **F14 builds VOICE-CLARITY Phase B itself:** the gate scaffold, the ladder, and the per-(word, voice) quarantine.
- **D-NS3a.** Single-voice ladder: re-render at rate 0.75; then re-render with a 150 ms SSML pause before the word; then quarantine. A second voice is a separate decision. The gate scores the whole clip, lead-in included ("The word is worm" against "The word is were").
- **D-NS6.** A/B the Google effects profile, headphone vs handset, on the 50 listen-test words. Eric listens on the phone speaker and on earbuds. Switch the served English audio to handset, and re-render the cache, only if handset wins on the speaker and is no worse on earbuds.
- **Sim.** ffmpeg chain: high-pass around 400 Hz, gentle roll-off above 8 kHz, light compression. Fitted to Eric's phone later (A14.6).
- **D-NS7.** Forced scoring runs in a gate-only Python venv on the Mac:
  - pinned torch (Apple GPU) and openai-whisper `medium.en` (code and weights MIT);
  - teacher-forced decoding: the summed token log-probs of each candidate transcript;
  - offline only, never on the server or in the app.
  - wav2vec2 is not installed unless D-NS5 step 2 is reached.
- **D-NS8.** Eric hand-keys the five words without pronunciation keys (circumlocution, amanuensis, chiaroscuro, fuchsia, onomatopoeia), tagged `source: manual`.
  - Neighbors stay generated.
  - Until a word is keyed it is outside the gate's pass list and out of Easy and Medium (fail closed). All five are Hard or Expert today.

## Definitions (F15)

- **D-SRC1a.** Princeton WordNet's first sense for the word's part of speech may be used as prompt input. Its glosses never ship verbatim. Wiktionary stays check-only.
- **D-SRC4.** Generation pipeline:
  - Model and limits: a current Sonnet-class Claude model through the Batch API, at low temperature, with a hard cap of $25.
  - Pilot: 100 rows first, then project the full cost. Over the cap means stop and ask.
  - Inputs: headword, part of speech, WordNet sense, tier. Nothing else.
  - Validator rules, enforced in code:
    - The Definition is 15 words or fewer and doesn't contain the headword or its stem.
    - The Sentence is 6–14 words and uses the headword exactly once.
    - Easy and Medium text reads at about grade 4 or below.
    - Both fields pass the safety prescreen and the profanity list.
  - Two candidates per field; the first that passes is kept. If both fail, the field stays dark and is listed.
  - Every prompt is logged, and the A15.10 canary check runs on that log.
  - New sentence audio goes through the same TTS render path.
- **Gig A brief.** Eric's July template goes into `docs/cc/`; it is not on this Mac yet. CC adapts it for English: separate Definition and Sentence severity columns, decoys, flag-don't-rewrite. Eric approves it.
  - The rater is a native English speaker on Fiverr with teaching or editing experience.
  - Eric also rates every Kid-visible row.
- **D-DEF12.** One gate, `canRender(row, field)`. All eight render paths call it, and so does sentence audio. A grep check fails CI if a path bypasses it.
- **D-DEF13.** One `dealablePool(view)` function, used by both the Definition Match pool check and the dealer. Test: any view that passes the check can deal 10 rounds. This freeze fix ships on its own, ahead of the rest.
- **N = 40** reviewed rows per tier (the existing `POOL_FLOOR`).
